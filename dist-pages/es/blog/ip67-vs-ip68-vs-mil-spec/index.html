IP67 vs IP68 vs MIL-SPEC: Which Standard Do You Need?

By KeXinMaterials Technical Team | 2026-08-29

Decodificando estándares de protección. Costos vs rendimiento.

Choosing the right protection standard is one of the most common dilemmas for B2B buyers of protective cases. This guide decodes IP67, IP68, and MIL-SPEC standards, with practical recommendations for different applications.

IP Rating System Explained

The Ingress Protection (IP) rating system uses two digits: first digit = solid particle protection (0-6), second digit = liquid protection (0-9). Higher numbers indicate better protection.

  • IP67: Dust tight + temporary immersion (1m depth, 30 minutes). Most common for commercial cases.
  • IP68: Dust tight + continuous immersion (typically 3m depth, manufacturer-specified duration). Required for marine and diving applications.
  • IP69K: Dust tight + high-pressure, high-temperature water jets. Used in food processing and automotive wash-down environments.

MIL-SPEC vs IP Rating

MIL-SPEC (MIL-STD-810H) is a US military standard covering 28 environmental test methods including: drop, vibration, shock, temperature, humidity, rain, sand, dust, immersion, altitude, fungus, salt fog. It's significantly more comprehensive than IP ratings.

Key MIL-STD-810H methods for protective cases:

  • Method 500.6: Low pressure (altitude)
  • Method 501.7: High temperature (+70°C operational, +85°C storage)
  • Method 502.7: Low temperature (-40°C operational, -51°C storage)
  • Method 506.5: Rain (blowing rain, drip, freezing rain)
  • Method 507.6: Humidity (95% RH, 60°C)
  • Method 509.7: Salt fog (corrosion resistance)
  • Method 510.7: Sand and dust
  • Method 512.7: Immersion (1m for 30 minutes minimum)
  • Method 514.8: Vibration (loose cargo, secured cargo, helicopter)
  • Method 516.8: Shock (functional shock, crash hazard shock, bench handling)

Application Recommendations

Choose IP67 when: Your equipment is occasionally exposed to rain, splashes, or brief submersion. Typical applications: outdoor photography, drone transport, instrument cases, electronics. IP67 is the sweet spot for most B2B applications, balancing cost and protection.

Choose IP68 when: Your equipment is continuously submerged or exposed to high-pressure water. Typical applications: marine electronics, diving equipment, underwater photography, scientific instruments. Expect 30-50% cost premium over IP67.

Choose MIL-SPEC when: Your equipment is used in military, tactical, emergency response, or extreme industrial environments. Typical applications: military radio, tactical electronics, field laptops, weapons accessories. Expect 2-4x cost premium over IP67.

Cost vs Performance Tradeoffs

Reference pricing for similar-sized case (40x30x15cm): IP67 = $45-85, IP68 = $80-150, MIL-SPEC = $120-280. The price gap reflects additional testing, materials, and quality control requirements.

Common Buyer Mistakes

Mistake 1: Specifying MIL-SPEC when IP67 would suffice. Adds 200% cost without operational benefit.

Mistake 2: Choosing IP68 for occasional water exposure. IP67 is adequate and cheaper.

Mistake 3: Skipping IP rating verification. IP ratings from suppliers should be backed by accredited lab test reports (SGS, TUV, BV).

Recommendation

For 80% of B2B applications, IP67 is the right choice. Specify IP68 only for true submersion requirements. Specify MIL-SPEC only for military, tactical, or extreme industrial environments. Always request test reports and consider ordering samples for in-house validation before bulk orders.
